## The Walk

### 1. Begin at Griesgasse: Salzburg's Working Edge

Stand outside **Griesgasse 13** with the river behind the buildings and the steep Mönchsberg rising on the opposite side of the Old Town. Salzburg's famous center occupies a narrow strip squeezed between mountain and river. That geography explains why the lanes are compact, why buildings grew upward, and why shortcuts often pass straight through buildings rather than around them.

Before leaving, save the Bounce booking code offline and set a phone alarm for **4:45 PM**. Bag pickup is the one fixed deadline today.

Walk southeast on Griesgasse, then turn into one of the passages leading toward Getreidegasse.

### 2. Getreidegasse: Read the Street Above Eye Level

Getreidegasse is Salzburg's main pedestrian spine. Ignore the modern storefronts for a moment and look upward:

- Ornamental wrought-iron guild signs project over the lane.
- Upper-floor windows become progressively smaller.
- Old façades carry dates, former owners' names, and occasional religious symbols.
- Narrow **through-houses** connect Getreidegasse with parallel streets and courtyards.

The street's name sounds like the German word for grain, but it probably evolved from older words associated with trotting or moving along the lane. The best surviving urban trick is the passage through **Getreidegasse 3**, which connects to Universitätsplatz. Step into at least one passageway; these are not merely shopping arcades but a practical response to a city with very little room to spread sideways.

### 3. Mozart's Birthplace: No. 9

Stop at the yellow façade of **Getreidegasse 9**, facing Hagenauerplatz. Wolfgang Amadé Mozart was born here on January 27, 1756. The Mozart family rented a third-floor apartment in this building from the Hagenauer family and lived here for 26 years.

For today's core tour, appreciate the exterior and the crowded commercial street that surrounded Mozart's childhood. The museum is worthwhile for strong Mozart interest, but it takes about an hour and is not barrier-free. In July and August it is currently scheduled to open from 8:30 AM to 7:00 PM, so it can be added later in the afternoon if energy and time allow.

Look toward the river through Hagenauerplatz. This opening once led toward a city gate used for river access and watering livestock—a useful reminder that elegant Salzburg was also a practical trading town.

### 4. Universitätsplatz: Lunch and Market-City Salzburg

Use a through-house to reach **Universitätsplatz**. This is the best point to pause for lunch because it keeps the group close to the route without committing to a long sit-down meal. Look for market stalls if operating, a bakery, sausages, sandwiches, or a casual café.

The large church here is the **Kollegienkirche**, the university church. Its pale Baroque mass opens into a square that feels dramatically larger than the tight lanes only a few steps away. Salzburg repeatedly creates this effect: compressed alley, hidden passage, sudden theatrical square.

After lunch, return toward Getreidegasse and continue east to Alter Markt.

### 5. Alter Markt: Salzburg's Old Commercial Living Room

**Alter Markt** is more intimate than the grand prince-archbishop squares ahead. Pause here for façades, shopfronts, and the sense of a civic center built for trade rather than ceremony.

If the group wants a traditional café stop, this is the logical area. Keep it brief now or save it for the return. The day's most impressive architecture is still ahead.

Continue through the lanes toward Residenzplatz.

### 6. Residenzplatz: Power Made Visible

Step into **Residenzplatz** and stop near the center rather than hugging the buildings. This was not an organically grown medieval square. Prince-Archbishop Wolf Dietrich reshaped the area as a statement of authority, and dozens of medieval buildings were cleared to create the open space.

Turn slowly and identify the four sides:

- The **Old Residence**, the former seat of Salzburg's prince-archbishops
- The **New Residence**, with its Glockenspiel tower
- Salzburg Cathedral
- A disciplined wall of townhouses completing the stage set

The great marble **Residenz Fountain** is among the most important Baroque fountains in Europe. Its sequence of horses, muscular figures, sea creatures, and water rising upward is less a quiet decoration than a piece of civic theater.

### 7. Mozartplatz: A Monument with Something Underfoot

Walk a few steps to neighboring **Mozartplatz**. The Mozart monument was unveiled in 1842, a year later than intended because workers discovered a Roman mosaic beneath the square. Mozart's sons attended the dedication.

This stop connects three versions of Salzburg: the Roman settlement below ground, the prince-archbishop city surrounding the square, and the 19th-century city turning Mozart into its international symbol.

### 8. Salzburg Cathedral: Look for Continuity, Not Just Grandeur

Enter **Salzburg Cathedral** if it is open to visitors. The current Baroque building was consecrated in 1628, but the religious history of this site reaches much farther back.

Inside, focus on a few things rather than trying to absorb everything:

- The huge dome and the controlled white-and-dark interior
- The multiple organs positioned around the crossing
- The baptismal font associated with Mozart's baptism
- The contrast between the broad ceremonial interior and the tight medieval lanes outside

This was not simply a parish church. Salzburg's rulers were prince-archbishops, combining religious and political authority. The cathedral and Residence face one another because church and government were two parts of the same ruling system.

### 9. Kapitelplatz: The Fortress Reveal

Exit toward **Kapitelplatz**. This broad square provides one of the clearest upward views of Hohensalzburg Fortress. The fortress is tomorrow's major visit, so today is about orientation: locate the funicular route and understand how completely the castle dominates the Old Town below.

The large golden sphere and modern art provide an intentionally jarring contrast with the cathedral and fortress. This is also a useful restroom, snack, and regrouping zone.

Do not ride the funicular today unless the entire schedule is running far ahead. The fortress has protected time on Day 7.

## Return Route

From Kapitelplatz, return through **Residenzplatz**, then choose either:

1. **Direct route:** Alter Markt → Getreidegasse → passage to Griesgasse, or
2. **Quieter route:** Goldgasse and the smaller lanes before rejoining Getreidegasse.

Aim to reach Griesgasse 13 by **5:40 PM**, collect the bags at 6:00 PM, and walk west to the apartment at **Gstättengasse 25**.

## Optional Additions

Choose at most one unless the group is moving quickly:

- **Mozart's Birthplace museum:** About one hour; strongest choice for music interest
- **Café stop at Alter Markt:** Best low-effort break
- **Extra through-house exploration:** Free, quick, and uniquely Salzburg
- **Brief river view:** Use a passage near Hagenauerplatz, look across the Salzach, then return to the route

Save the fortress, Mirabell Gardens, and an extended Salzach walk for Day 7.
